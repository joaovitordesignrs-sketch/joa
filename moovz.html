Case Study

Moovz

Turning small studios into high-standard gyms — with AI-powered class creation and a live workout player.

Role
Product Designer · UX Researcher
Year
2025
Type
B2B2C · Web App
Segment
FitTech
01 — Market Context

A model that works — but only if you can afford it

There's a business model that works really well in large gyms — structured classes, standardized experience, predictable results regardless of which instructor is in the room. F45 does this. Orange Theory does this. The problem is that this model, until now, required a level of infrastructure that small studios simply don't have.

Moovz saw an opening in this gap. And I was the designer responsible for turning that bet into a product.

Blue Ocean Strategy — Kim & Mauborgne

The bet was not to compete with consolidated gym management software, but to create an entirely new category: the operating system for the class experience itself.

02 — The Problem

The problem has three owners

What seemed like an operational cost problem — qualified instructors are expensive — was actually three overlapping problems.

For the studio owner: high personnel costs, inconsistent class experience between instructors, and a growth ceiling determined by the number of available professionals.

For the instructor: beyond teaching, planning new and varied workouts for each group is work that happens off the clock, without proportional compensation.

For the student: workout quality depends on which instructor showed up today. This creates an inconsistent experience that doesn't build loyalty.

The solution needed to solve all three at once — and that's what made designing this product complex.

Studio Owner

High personnel costs, inconsistent class experience between instructors, and a growth ceiling determined by the number of available professionals.

Instructor

Planning overload — creating new, varied, well-structured classes for each group is invisible work that drains time and energy.

Student

Inconsistent experience quality, dependent on which instructor is available that day.

The Ceiling

One instructor = one class at a time. Scaling the business means scaling the payroll linearly.

"The bet was to create the operating system of the class — not another gym management tool."

The strategic positioning that defined every product decision

03 — The Solution

Five functional layers, one unified platform

Moovz designed a B2B2C web application — sold to the studio owner, but the final experience benefits students directly and simplifies the instructor's work. The solution was structured in five layers, each solving a piece of the puzzle.

04 — AI-Powered Class Creation

AI as scaffolding, not replacement

The instructor creates classes with AI assistance — which suggests exercise sequences, work/rest timings, and workout structures based on parameters defined by the professional themselves.

The most important design decision here was keeping control with the instructor. The AI suggests, they decide. This respects Nielsen's Heuristic #3 — User Control and Freedom and, more importantly, respects the technical knowledge of a professional who spent years studying for this. The AI serves as scaffolding — reduces planning effort without replacing human judgment.

Step 1 — AI Assistance

The instructor defines parameters and the AI suggests a complete class structure — configurable before confirmation.

Step 2 — Class Generated

The AI-generated class with exercises, timing and rest periods — fully editable by the instructor.

05 — Weekly & Daily Schedule

Information density meets visual clarity

With classes ready, the owner organizes the schedule. The system automates programming — times, rooms, available instructors. The interface needed to be dense enough to give managerial visibility without being complicated to operate.

Schedule Overview

The programming dashboard with weekly schedule and class previews at a glance.

Weekly Grid

Day-by-day schedule with time slots and class assignment — designed for Gestalt grouping and quick scanning.

06 — Pre-Class Display

Reducing uncertainty before the class starts

Before class starts, students see on a screen what's coming — which workout is scheduled and exercise demonstrations. This solves something few people name but every student feels: the anxiety of not knowing what's going to happen. Reducing uncertainty before an aversive stimulus (intense workout) increases the willingness to face it.

TV Display — Pre-Class

Students see the next class, time, and exercise previews before it starts — reducing uncertainty and onboarding new members.

07 — Live Class Player

Designed for 4-meter readability under movement

The instructor hits play. From there, exercises appear on video with sequence, timing, and instructions. A single instructor can run a full class with the same experience quality as a structured chain.

The design of this screen had an unusual constraint: it needs to work being read from 4 meters away, by someone in movement, in an environment with loud music and multiple visual stimuli. Every decision about typography, color, and hierarchy was made with this context in mind.

Live Player — Exercise View

Full-screen exercise demonstration with countdown timer, exercise name, and rep counter — readable from 4+ meters.

Live Player — Timer Mode

Large-format countdown timer with circuit navigation — designed for high cognitive load environments with loud music and movement.

"Every unnecessary element on screen is a distraction that costs attention in a moment where attention is scarce."

The design principle for the live class player

08 — B2B2C Model

Three users, one platform

One of the biggest design challenges was balancing three personas with radically different needs on the same platform. Each interface was designed for the specific use context of each persona.

  • Studio Owner — management panel (desktop, office environment). Needs control, visibility and operational simplicity.
  • Instructor — class creation + live player (TV, gym floor, 3-5m distance). Needs creative autonomy and reduced planning effort.
  • Student — pre-class display (passive viewing, no interaction). Needs clarity, motivation and a high-standard class experience.
09 — Results & Project State

A product in development — no fabricated metrics

Moovz Class was conceived and designed with complete flows and delivered interfaces. Adoption data and impact will be built with the launch. The concrete result here is the product itself — and it speaks for itself when well presented.

Delivered Product

The result is the product itself — AI class creation, automated scheduling, pre-class display and live player, designed end-to-end.

10 — Learnings

You can't optimize for just one persona in B2B2C

Working on a B2B2C product exposed a challenge that rarely appears in B2C: you can't prioritize only the end user. The purchase decision is the studio owner's, but retention depends on the student's experience. Optimizing for one persona without considering the others can solve one problem and create three new ones.

The second learning was about extreme use context. Designing an interface that needs to work readable from 4 meters away, by a moving instructor, with loud music — is an exercise that forces absolute clarity. Every unnecessary element on screen costs attention in a moment where attention is scarce.

"Optimizing for one persona without considering the others can solve one problem and create three new ones."

The core lesson of B2B2C product design
